| **Status** | Proposed / Accepted / Superseded by #NNNN / Deprecated |
| **Date** | YYYY-MM-DD |

| **Affects** | Which docs, directories, or milestones this constrains |

## Context

What is the situation that forces a decision? What constraints apply — product
requirements from the spec, team size, timeline, existing commitments?

State the forces in tension. A decision with no trade-off did not need an ADR.

## Decision

The decision, in one or two sentences, stated actively:

> We will …

Then any specifics needed to act on it — the rule, the boundary, the convention.

## Consequences

### What this makes easier

- …

### What this makes harder

- …

Be honest here. An ADR listing only benefits is advocacy, not a record, and it
is useless to the person later trying to work out whether the trade-off still
holds.

### What this forecloses

Options this rules out, or makes expensive to revisit later.

## Alternatives considered

### Alternative A

**What it was.** Why it was rejected — specifically, not "it was worse".

### Alternative B

**What it was.** Why it was rejected.

> The rejected alternatives are the most valuable part of an ADR. Anyone can see
> what was chosen by reading the code; only this file records what was weighed
> against it.

## Revisit when

Concrete conditions that would justify reopening this. A change in team size, a
scale threshold, a dependency's maturity, a product requirement arriving.

## References

- Links to specs, docs, issues, or external material that informed the decision.
